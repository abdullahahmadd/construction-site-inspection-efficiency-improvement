## Overview

This project improves the efficiency of the construction site QA/QC inspection process using the Six Sigma DMAIC methodology in a Saudi construction environment. It analyzes operational inefficiencies affecting inspection approvals, process delays, rework activities, and documentation management, then identifies root causes and improvement opportunities to enhance inspection workflow efficiency and operational performance.

---

## Business Problem

The construction site inspection process was experiencing several operational inefficiencies, including delayed inspection approvals, high inspection rejection rates, repeated rework activities, incomplete inspection documentation, communication gaps between site and QA/QC teams, and slow approval response times. These issues resulted in construction delays, increased labor costs, reduced operational efficiency, and reduced project productivity.

---

## Objectives

- Reduce inspection turnaround time and improve approval efficiency
- Reduce inspection rejection rates and minimize rework activities
- Improve documentation accuracy across the inspection workflow
- Enhance coordination between construction and QA/QC teams
- Identify operational bottlenecks affecting the inspection process end to end

## Methodology

**Define**
Developed a Project Charter and Team Charter to establish objectives, business impact, stakeholders, process boundaries, and improvement goals, plus a SIPOC diagram to map Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, and Customers.

**Measure**
Built a Data Collection Plan identifying the operational KPIs, inspection performance measures, process data requirements, and data collection responsibilities needed to baseline the process.

**Analyze**
Created a detailed QA/QC inspection process map covering inspection request flow, approval activities, scheduling, documentation handling, and rework processes. Performed root cause analysis using a Fishbone Diagram to identify what was driving inspection delays, communication gaps, documentation errors, and repeated rework, and developed hypotheses to test whether targeted process changes would materially improve performance.

**Improve**
Identified improvement opportunities targeting workflow efficiency, inspection scheduling, documentation standardization, and communication between operational teams.

**Control**
Proposed operational standardization recommendations to sustain the improvements once implemented.

## Key Findings

- Manual workflow activities were a direct driver of inspection delays, indicating the process bottleneck was procedural rather than caused by inspector availability or site conditions.
- Incomplete documentation was consistently linked to approval bottlenecks, meaning documentation standardization would address delays at their source rather than downstream in the approval queue.
- Communication gaps between site and QA/QC teams affected scheduling efficiency specifically, pointing to a coordination fix rather than a resourcing fix.
- Repeated inspection failures were the main driver of elevated rework frequency, confirming that reducing first-time rejection rates would have a compounding effect on overall rework volume.
- The absence of process standardization was identified as a root cause underlying multiple symptoms (delays, rework, documentation errors) rather than a separate issue on its own.

---

## Business Recommendations

1. Implement a digital inspection tracking system to replace manual workflow steps identified as delay drivers.
2. Standardize inspection request documentation to close the gap linked to approval bottlenecks.
3. Improve scheduling coordination between site and QA/QC departments to address the communication-driven delays.
4. Develop QA/QC checklist templates to reduce first-time inspection rejection rates.
5. Reduce approval bottlenecks through targeted workflow optimization rather than broad process overhaul.
